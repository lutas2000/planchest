# Interview script: context

Writes `CONTEXT.md`. Ask in this order. Each round is one message.

## Round 1. What it is, and for whom

1. In one sentence, what does the product do, and for whom? (Example: "Stride records runs and keeps runners on a weekly goal.")
2. Who uses it? Name each role and what that person is trying to get done. One line per role. (Default: one role, "user".)
3. What does each role already know, and what do they not? (Example: "Runners know pace and splits; they do not know what a GPS fix is.")
4. Where is it used: desk, phone in hand, phone in a pocket, a shared screen, in a hurry, at leisure?

→ Audience and roles.

## Round 2. Vocabulary

5. List the five to ten nouns the product is made of (the objects a user creates, views, or acts on). Example: run, goal, split, route.
6. For each, is there a word you do not want used for it? (Example: "run, not workout, not activity".)
7. What states can the main objects be in? (Example: "a run is active, paused, or saved".)
8. Which words does your audience use that the industry does not, or the other way round?

→ Vocabulary table, with Don't say filled from 6.

## Round 3. Facts that shape screens

9. What is irreversible? What needs a confirmation, and what must never have one?
10. Limits and quantities: how many of each object is typical, how many is a lot? (Example: "a run has 5–40 splits; a history has hundreds of runs.")
11. Units, currencies, time zones, number formats the product must get right.
12. Anything that happens offline, slowly, or in the background that the screens have to show?

→ Domain facts an agent needs.

## Round 4. What may not be said

13. Legal or regulatory limits on copy: health, finance, children, guarantees.
14. Competitor names, superlatives, or claims the company avoids.
15. Anything else a reviewer has rejected before?

→ Forbidden words and claims.
